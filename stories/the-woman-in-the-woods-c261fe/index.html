THE WOMAN IN THE WOODS

Betrayed by her own people, an immortal woman transforms into a powerful force whose anger is felt through the winds and storms of the Earth.

Published 26 November 2026
THE WOMAN IN THE WOODS

A long time ago, there was a village called Lafete — spelled L-A-F-E-T-E. It was small and quiet, but what happened there would later explain many strange things that happen on Earth. This story is not just about a village. It is about why winds blow at night, why leaves shake, why storms rise suddenly, and why shadows sometimes seem alive.

In Lafete, the rains did not come one year. The sun burned the land, rivers dried, and crops died. People grew weak. Sickness spread, and many died. Among them lived a woman, kind and strong, who cared for her neighbors and her family. But she watched helplessly as her husband grew weak and died, as friends vanished, and as the village became empty.

Before she ran to seek the gods, she was pregnant. She carried the life of her unborn child, a small hope in the dying village. But she knew that if she did not act, both she and the child would die. She ran into the forest that bordered Lafete, deep where no villager dared to follow, to meet the gods themselves.

Under the tall trees, she prayed to the gods of wind, rain, and earth. She asked for her life — and unknowingly, she asked for immortality. The gods granted her wish. She survived. But everyone she loved, including her husband and neighbors, perished. Generations passed, yet she remained, unaging, trapped between life and death.

The villagers grew afraid. Even the king, once her friend, feared her. One night, they lured her deep into the forest, stacked wood around her, and set her on fire. She screamed as the flames climbed her body, but the fire could not kill her. Pain twisted her soul, but it also gave her power. The gods answered her cries: she became one with the wind, the trees, and the storms. She could vanish into the air and yet take human form again.

When she returned, she gave birth to her child, but her heart had changed. The world had betrayed her. She buried the child alive beneath the forest floor. The child’s spirit became part of her, feeding her power and connecting her to the forest and the wind.

The first time the wind rose after that night, it howled like her screams, shaking trees, rattling leaves, and chilling the village. Animals ran in terror. Lafete was no longer just a village — it became a symbol of Earth itself. Every gust of wind, every trembling leaf, every storm, every earthquake or wildfire today… it is tied to that first betrayal and her transformation.

The woman did not stop at haunting the village. Her anger and grief consumed her. She attacked Lafete. Storms tore through the streets. Fires spread where the sun was hot. Rain fell in sudden floods. Winds lifted roofs, snapped trees, and scattered animals. Villagers ran and hid. Nothing could stop her. Lafete became a ghost of its former self, abandoned and ruined.

But Lafete was only the beginning. She rose with the wind, spreading her power across mountains, rivers, and oceans. In far deserts, sudden storms twisted sand into tall pillars. Across the seas, waves rose taller than ships, crashing without warning on distant shores. In frozen lands, icy wind tore across plains, shaking the few trees that dared to grow. In forests far away, leaves quivered and animals scattered, fleeing an invisible presence.

Everywhere she passed, the air carried her memory: a chill in the night, a soft whisper through the leaves, a shadow crossing the land. And even now, every hurricane, sudden wind, or tremor carries a trace of her, a reminder that the Woman In The Woods walks the Earth. Lafete may have been small, but her power now touches the corners of the world.

Travelers who enter forests, mountains, or plains sometimes return changed. Their eyes are distant, their voices hollow, as though part of them was left behind in the wind. Some never return, swallowed by shadows, leaving only footprints that vanish after a single rain.

Even today, in Lafete Village, the wind blows through the trees at night, shaking leaves, stirring branches, and chilling the air. The villagers say it is the Woman In The Woods walking among them, moving unseen, her eyes still glowing from the fire that was meant to kill her.

Even now, on quiet nights, children in villages far from Lafete hear the wind whisper through the trees. Leaves shake as if touched by invisible hands. The air grows colder without reason. Animals pause, ears pricked, staring into empty shadows.

Some say that if you step outside when the wind moves strongest, you can feel her passing, a soft brush like a hand, a faint rustle in the branches. The trees seem to lean closer, the clouds move faster, and the night feels alive.

And if a child dares to ask aloud, “Who is there?” the wind may answer with a soft, mournful sigh. The Woman In The Woods is still walking, still watching, still carrying the power of Lafete and the Earth in her steps.

No one knows exactly where she will appear next. Perhaps in the mountains, in the forest, or across the oceans. But every gust, every storm, every trembling leaf is a reminder:

“She was once one of us. She is still among us. And she will not die.”

Lafete was small, but its story is the story of Earth itself, a warning that her anger, grief, and immortality are still alive today, moving across the world, carried by wind and storm, forever.

THE ERADICATION OF DRAGONS

A legendary war between dragons and dinosaurs reveals the mysterious origins of the tortoise's shell and the power of sacrifice.

Published 21 November 2026
THE ERADICATION OF DRAGON



Peter (Petros) Bamigbaye

Human beings are curious by nature.
We look at the world and ask why—why the tortoise carries a shell, why stories speak of dragons, why myths appear wherever people have lived and wondered.

This book exists because those questions never truly disappeared.
They were simply waiting for answers told in a language older than fact.

Within these pages are explanations shaped as stories, not to (and to) prove what existed, and to explain what was believed, feared, and remembered.

Curiosity is the beginning.
This book is the response.







THE ERADICATION OF DRAGON

A long, long time ago, before people walked the earth, there were dragons and dinosaurs. Dragons ruled the skies, their scales shining like fire and their wings casting shadows across the land. Dinosaurs ruled the earth, strong and proud. For a time, the world was peaceful. Rivers sparkled, forests stretched for miles, and every creature lived freely.

But peace does not last forever.

The dragons grew proud and greedy. They looked down on all the smaller creatures and said, “Why should we share this world with the weak? The earth belongs to us, and we will take it all.”

And so they attacked.

First, they attacked the animals. They stomped on deer and rabbits, crushing them under their claws. They breathed fire across forests, burning trees and scaring animals from their homes. Wolves and foxes ran as fast as they could, but dragons chased them, swooping down and snapping with claws and teeth. Birds tried to escape, darting through the trees, but many were caught by fire or crushed by falling trees. Fish leapt from rivers to escape the heat, but some boiled in the water as dragons scorched it with their breath.

Some animals were clever and escaped. A rabbit dashed into a hollow log just before a dragon’s claw smashed the ground where it had been. A deer leapt into a river and swam to safety. A fox hid under a pile of stones while a dragon stomped near it. But many were not so lucky. The dragons killed for power, not for hunger, and soon the forests and plains grew quiet and empty.

The dinosaurs watched all of this. At first, they stayed back, seeing the dragons’ cruelty. But when they realized how many animals had been killed, how much of the earth had been destroyed, their hearts grew heavy with anger. “This is too much,” they said. “The dragons must be stopped, or nothing will survive.”

And so they decided to fight.

The dragons noticed the dinosaurs gathering. “Ha!” they roared. “Land creatures cannot challenge us! We are fire! We are the sky!”

But the dinosaurs were brave. They charged forward, shaking the ground with their feet. They fought the dragons with all their strength. The dragons breathed fire and struck with claws and tails. Many dinosaurs fell, crushed or burned. The battle spread across forests, rivers, and plains.

In a hidden cave, two tiny tortoises watched the war. They were scared and small. They hid under a big stone, trembling as dragons killed and burned everything outside. They stayed quiet and waited, hoping the world would survive.

But hope was coming.

In a secret valley untouched by fire, a special egg cracked open. From it hatched a dinosaur unlike any other. It had wings of living flame, bright and magical, like dancing fire. This was the Winged One, born to fight the dragons and restore balance.

The Winged One grew fast. It learned to fly higher than the clouds. Its flames could burn through dragon scales. Its roar shook mountains and rivers. Alone, it struck fear into the dragons. One by one, dragons fell, though many still fought fiercely.

The dragons were clever and cruel. They stomped, clawed, and breathed fire. They killed smaller dinosaurs where they stood and still attacked any remaining animals. One dragon grabbed a deer in its claws, but the deer darted free at the last second. A rabbit ran under a hollow log to escape being crushed. Birds flew between dragons’ claws, narrowly escaping the flames.

The Winged One knew it could not fight alone forever. It looked at the dinosaurs around it, many wounded, some falling. Then it made a choice.

With a cry that shook the sky, the Winged One tore its magical wings apart and shared them with the other dinosaurs. Wherever its fire touched, dinosaurs grew wings, strong and magical. Suddenly, dinosaurs could fly! They rose into the sky and joined the fight against the dragons.

The war became the Great War of Fire and Sky. Dragons and dinosaurs battled high above the clouds. Fire lit the night like twin suns. Wings clashed. Claws met scales. Dinosaurs swooped and dodged dragons’ fire. Some dragons fell into rivers, others were trapped in forests, and a few fled to distant mountains.

Below, the tortoises in their cave felt the earth tremble and the roar of the battle. They waited patiently, knowing their time would come. At last, when the skies grew quiet and the fires faded, they came out. But the stone they had hidden beneath had fused with their bodies. They could not remove it, so they carried it with them. When they had children, their children were born with the stone on their backs. That is why tortoises have shells even today.

No one knows if all dragons were defeated. Some say the battle still continues high in the clouds. If dinosaurs win, they will return to the earth and bring peace. If the dragons win, fire will fall again on the world.

Whenever you see a tortoise walking slowly with its shell, remember: it carries the story of fire, courage, and magic. And somewhere in the sky, dragons and dinosaurs are still fighting, waiting for the day the war will end.
